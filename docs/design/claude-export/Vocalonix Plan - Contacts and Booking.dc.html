Contacts & Booking

Today a conversation is either a phone number or “website visitor”. Nothing joins the two up, so the agent meets everyone as a stranger and the owner reads a log of digits. Two modules fix that: Contacts gives every conversation a person behind it, and Booking gives the agent a diary it actually understands.

How a conversation finds its person
1Phone call — caller ID matched exactly to a channel. No match and a contact is created on the spot, unnamed.
2Website call — widget session id, then anything the visitor gives (name, number, email) links it to an existing contact.
3Logged-in site — the widget can be handed a customer id and email, and that beats every other signal.
4Suggested merge — two records sharing a number or email surface as “same person?”. Never merged silently.
Getting the name — three routes, one record
The widget asks Optional field before the call connects: “Your name (optional)”. Three settings — don’t ask, ask, require. Never blocks the call button. SOURCE: VISITOR · HIGH TRUST
The agent hears it Mid-conversation, if a name is said, the agent calls update_contact and the record renames itself while the call is still running. SOURCE: AGENT · SPELLING UNSURE
The diary knows A matching patient or client in the connected system wins over both, and its spelling is treated as canonical. SOURCE: SYNC · CANONICAL
Tool: update_contact name? · phone? · email? · note? · tag?
· Only fills blanks. Overwriting a name it already has needs the caller to correct it out loud. · Never asks for the name twice, and never asks at all if the widget already got it. · Won’t take a third party’s name (“I’m calling for my mum”) as the caller’s own. · Every write is stamped with source, confidence and the transcript line that caused it. · Uncertain spellings land as Sarah (heard) and queue up under “confirm these”.
In the transcript It’s Marcus Bell, I came in last spring. CONTACT UPDATED · +44 7700 900482 → MARCUS BELL · MATCHED 1 PAST VISIT Good to hear from you again, Marcus.
Where it shows Conversation list and header lead with the name, and the number becomes the subtitle. New nav item: Contacts, searchable, one page per person.
What it unlocks Returning-caller greetings, “same slot as last time”, no-show and deposit rules, blocked numbers, and a real customer list the owner owns.
Privacy Contacts belong to the business, exportable and deletable in one action, retention window is a setting, and the widget name field carries its own consent line.
02

Booking

small, but enough for a clinic, a salon or a studio

One model, four moving parts. Every vertical we sell to is a combination of these — we ship the parts, onboarding picks the preset. It stands alone for businesses with no diary software, and mirrors the real system for those who have one.

Services Duration, price, buffer before and after, deposit, who can perform it, and whether the agent may book it at all.
Resources People and things. A booking can need both — a stylist and a chair, a therapist and a room, or a room on its own.
Availability Opening hours, per-resource rotas, holidays, lunch, slot granularity, and how far ahead or how last-minute you take work.
Policies Deposits, cancellation window, no-show handling, reminders, and who is allowed to book without a card.

Deliberately not in v1 Packages and memberships, payroll or commission, stock, multi-site routing, public self-serve booking pages, class check-in. The booking module exists so the agent can finish a call — not to replace the salon’s software.
03

What changes in what we’ve already built

Onboarding “Services & prices” grows durations and buffers. A new question after the diary step — who or what gets booked, prefilled by vertical. The widget step gains the optional-name toggle.
Conversations Rows lead with a name and a “3rd visit” marker. Transcript shows contact updates inline. “What it did” links to the booking, which can be moved from here.
Dashboard & Diary Diary becomes a real day view across resources. New numbers worth having: new versus returning, no-show rate, waitlist filled.
The brief Gains two lines the owner controls: how the agent greets someone it recognises, and whether it may say out loud what they booked last time.
Ship first Contact record, automatic matching, widget name field, update_contact, Contacts page.
Then Services with duration, resources, availability, find and hold and book. One vertical end to end before all three.
After Deposits, waitlist, reminders, the merge review queue, two-way sync with outside diaries.
04

Needs a decision from you

· Is our booking module the source of truth, or always a mirror of the business’s existing diary? It decides how hard conflicts hit. · Do we take deposits ourselves in v1, or hand off to a payment link the business already uses? · Which vertical goes first — clinic gives us the hardest rules, salon the fastest sale. · Should the widget ask for a phone number as well as a name? It roughly doubles the match rate and halves the fill rate.
